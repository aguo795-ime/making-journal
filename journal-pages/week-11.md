# Week 11
## In-Class Activities
### Journal Review
This week I reviewed my journal entries from Weeks 6–10 and reflected on how the project developed over time.
Looking back at the process, three key moments had the greatest influence on the direction of the project.

#### Key Moment 1: Exploring Multiple Concepts

F1: Development of the refined commuting route model using colour-coded pathway connections.

In Week 6 and Week 7, I explored several possible directions for representing commuting data. Testing different concepts helped me understand the strengths and limitations of each approach before selecting a final direction.

#### Key Moment 2: Selecting Comparing Commuting Experiences

F2: Exploring route structure and layout to improve clarity and visual organisation.
Choosing the Comparing Commuting Experiences concept helped give the project a clearer focus. This decision allowed me to combine route information, travel time, and personal experience within a single visual system.

#### Key Moment 3: Introducing Emotional Data

F3: Integrating route labels and supporting information into the physical prototype.

Adding emotional responses in Week 10 expanded the project beyond transport information. This helped communicate not only where people travel and how long journeys take, but also how different journeys can feel.
After reviewing the journal, I found that documenting experiments and reflections helped me better understand how the project evolved over time.

### Practice Consultations
This week I reflected on how I would explain the project during the upcoming Studio Consultation.
One area I felt confident discussing was the development process from early concepts to the final visualisation.
I was also able to explain how hand-drawn data visualisation influenced the project and why I chose to focus on everyday commuting experiences.
One area I would like to improve is explaining the broader significance of the project. I want to communicate more clearly why everyday commuting experiences can be meaningful forms of data and how visualisation can reveal aspects of daily life that are often overlooked.
The practice consultation helped me organise my thoughts and prepare for discussing the project more confidently.

#### Showcase Planning
This week I began preparing for the final showcase presentation.
I considered how the work would be displayed and how viewers would interact with the visualisation.
I also reviewed the overall layout of the project to ensure that key information could be understood clearly and quickly.
The showcase planning process helped me think about the project from the perspective of an audience rather than only as a designer.

## Independent Study
### Project Finalisation and Submission

F4: Final physical data visualisation prototype combining routes, labels, and comparative commuting data.

This week I focused on finalising the project and preparing all required documentation.

I reviewed the visualisation and made final adjustments to improve readability, consistency, and overall presentation quality.
I also reviewed my journal entries to ensure that the development process was clearly documented and supported with both visual and written evidence.

Reflecting on the project as a whole, I found that the most valuable part of the process was moving from broad exploration towards a focused visual outcome. The project developed gradually through concept testing, feedback, experimentation, and refinement.
The final visualisation compares commuting experiences between different students through route information, travel time, and emotional responses. By combining objective and personal data, the project aims to highlight how everyday journeys can differ from person to person.

#### Final Reflection
This project changed the way I think about data visualisation.
At the beginning, I mainly viewed data as information that could be measured and recorded. Through this project, I became more interested in how data can also represent personal experiences and everyday life.
The project demonstrated that commuting is not simply movement between locations. It is also an experience shaped by time, routine, and individual perception.
Preparing the final outcome helped me understand how visualisation can be used not only to communicate information, but also to communicate experience.
