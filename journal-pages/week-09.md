Project Statement: First Draft
My project explores how commuting experiences can be represented through hand-drawn data visualisation.
The project compares the journeys of three University of Auckland students travelling between home and the University of Auckland. By visualising routes, transport modes, and commuting times, the project aims to highlight how everyday journeys can differ between individuals.
The project combines public transport information with self-recorded commuting data to create a visual representation of everyday mobility.
Making Sprint
This week I began creating the first version of the commuting map.
I collected basic commuting information from three university students, including their travel routes, transport modes, and commuting times.

Figure 4: Route mapping sketch comparing commuting journeys from different student locations to the University of Auckland.
Using this information, I created an initial hand-drawn map showing the different journeys between home and the University of Auckland.
The focus of this version was on route structure and travel duration rather than detailed visual styling.
What worked well?

Figure 5: Data table summarising participant locations, transport modes, and total commuting times used for comparison and analysis.
The shared starting point at the University of Auckland made the routes easier to compare.
Different commuting times could be identified quickly.
What did not work well?
Some routes overlapped and became difficult to distinguish.
The map structure still needed refinement.
What did I learn?
Simple route organisation improves readability.
Clear labels are important when comparing multiple journeys.
