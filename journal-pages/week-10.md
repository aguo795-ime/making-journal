# Week 10
## In-Class Activities
### Progress Reports
This week I shared the latest version of my project with peers and received feedback on the first draft of my commuting visualisation.

![F1](../assets/week-010/Week10%20F1.png)

Figure 1: Early prototype exploring how physical materials can represent movement paths and connections.

The current project compares the commuting experiences of three University of Auckland students using route information and travel time data.

#### My feedback questions were:
Is the route comparison easy to understand?
Is the travel time information clear?
What additional information could strengthen the visualisation?
Several useful suggestions emerged during discussion.
One comment suggested that the project could communicate more of the experience behind the journey rather than only showing route and time information.
Another comment suggested that the visualisation could benefit from an additional layer of data that helps explain why different journeys feel different.
A further comment noted that the map structure was clear and easy to follow, but some areas felt visually empty.
Gallery Walk
During the gallery walk, I viewed projects from other groups and read both technical and conceptual feedback.
One observation I found useful was how other students layered multiple types of information within a single visual system while maintaining clarity.
This encouraged me to think about how additional information could be introduced without making the map difficult to read.
I also noticed several examples where colour was used to communicate information quickly and effectively.

#### Action Plan
After reviewing the feedback, I identified several actions for the next stage of development.
The most important feedback suggested that the project should communicate more than route and time information.
To address this, I decided to introduce emotional responses recorded during commuting journeys. This additional layer will help represent the personal experience behind each journey.

Independent Study
Project Development

![F3](../assets/week-010/Week10%20F3.png)

Figure 3: Testing pathway intersections to understand how different routes connect within the system.

This week I focused on developing the second version of the visualisation.
Using feedback from peers, I began incorporating emotional information into the commuting map.
In addition to route and travel time data, I experimented with simple symbols and colour coding to represent emotional responses experienced during different stages of the journey.

Examples included:
Relaxed
Neutral
Frustrated
Stressed

The goal was not to create a detailed emotional analysis, but to introduce a simple layer of personal experience that could be compared between journeys.

![F4](../assets/week-010/Week10%20F4.png)

Figure 4: Experimenting with alternative route configurations and network relationships.
I tested several visual approaches and found that simple symbols and limited colour use were easier to understand than highly detailed graphics.

Figure 5: Material testing and construction process used to develop the physical route mapping prototype.

This development helped the project move beyond purely functional transport information and towards a representation of lived commuting experience.

#### Reflection
Adding emotional information changed the way I viewed the project.
Previously, the visualisation mainly communicated where people travelled and how long the journey took.
Now it also begins to communicate how different journeys can feel.
This addition helped strengthen the connection between everyday experience and data visualisation while still maintaining the project's focus on comparison.
