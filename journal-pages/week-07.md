# Week 7
## In-Class Activities
### Concept Sketches
This week I continued developing the three concept directions that I identified in Week 6.
Concept 1 explored how commuting time could be represented through a hand-drawn timeline.
Concept 2 explored how public transport routes and stops could be visualised through a simplified network map.
Concept 3 explored how commuting experiences from different students could be compared through one visual system.

After reviewing these concepts, I felt that each direction offered different possibilities. Rather than selecting one immediately, I decided to test them further through a series of quick visual experiments.

What surprised me?
I realised that each concept highlighted a different aspect of commuting. Some focused more on time, while others focused more on movement and comparison.

What aligned with my ideas?
All three concepts connected back to my original interest in public transport and everyday experience.

What do I want to follow up on?
I want to explore which concept creates the clearest and most engaging visual representation of commuting data.

Making Sprint
For this making sprint, I created three small visual experiments based on my concept sketches.

#### Experiment 1: Daily Commuting Timeline

![F1](../assets/week-07/Week7%20F1.jpg)

I created a simple hand-drawn timeline showing different stages of a journey. This approach clearly communicated travel duration but provided limited information about location and movement.

#### Experiment 2: Public Transport Network Map

![F2](../assets/week-07/Week7%20F2.jpg)

I created a simplified map showing bus routes and stops. This approach communicated movement and route structure well, but it focused more on the transport system than individual experiences.
#### Experiment 3: Comparing Commuting Experiences

![F3](../assets/week-07/Week7%20F3.jpg)

I created a rough visual comparison between multiple commuting journeys. This approach allowed different routes and travel times to be viewed together within one visual system.

What worked well?
The comparison approach made it easier to see differences between journeys.

What did not work well?
Some layouts became crowded when too much information was added.

What did I learn?
Simple visual structures are easier to understand than complex layouts.

What If Variations
After testing the three concepts, I explored several alternative possibilities.

What if the project focused only on travel time?
What if the project focused only on public transport routes?
What if the project compared multiple commuting experiences?

The third option appeared the most interesting because it combined personal experience with transport information.
This direction also seemed flexible enough to include additional information in future stages if needed.

## Independent Study
### Project Development & Skill Building
This week I continued researching hand-drawn data visualisation and mapping methods.
I tested different ways of organising information, including timelines, route maps, and comparison layouts.
Through these experiments, I learnt that visual hierarchy is important. Too much information can quickly make a visualisation difficult to understand.

I also began thinking about how commuting information could be collected and compared between different people.
The project is gradually moving from broad exploration towards a clearer direction.
